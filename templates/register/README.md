# Register templates: records of processing and compliance dossier

> Status: draft, v0.2. Refs: [compliance-spec#14](https://github.com/C2-POLY/compliance-spec/issues/14) (v0.1), [compliance-spec#33](https://github.com/C2-POLY/compliance-spec/issues/33) (v0.2).

The templates of the document that the generator (the `register` mode of [compliance-action](https://github.com/C2-POLY/compliance-action)) writes in a compliance repo, at `documents/register.md`, and the rules to fill them. The generator implements these rules by hand, without a template library, and must produce the same bytes as the golden outputs for the Bistro Booking example. Tests check the golden files.

The version of the document is the `spec_version` of the compliance repo's `compliance.yaml` (see [compliance-repo.md](../../spec/compliance-repo.md#versions)):

| Compliance repo | Document | Template | Rules | Golden output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `spec_version: "0.1"` | Records of processing activities (GDPR Art. 30(1)) | [`register.md.tmpl`](register.md.tmpl) | [v0.1](#v01-the-records-of-processing) | [`tests/fixtures/v0.1/bistro-booking/documents/register.md`](../../tests/fixtures/v0.1/bistro-booking/documents/register.md) |
| `spec_version: "0.2"` | Compliance dossier, in English or French | [`register.en.md.tmpl`](register.en.md.tmpl), [`register.fr.md.tmpl`](register.fr.md.tmpl) | [v0.2](#v02-the-compliance-dossier) | `examples/bistro-booking/documents/register.md` (en) and `register.fr.md` (fr), generated by compliance-action and copied here byte for byte |

Both documents are prepared for review by the controller and its DPO. They do not certify compliance.

## v0.1: the records of processing

These rules are those of v0.1, unchanged. A v0.1 compliance repo keeps this register, byte for byte.

### Art. 30(1) items

| | Item (GDPR Art. 30(1)) |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | Name and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, of the joint controller, the controller's representative and the data protection officer |
| (b) | The purposes of the processing |
| (c) | A description of the categories of data subjects and of the categories of personal data |
| (d) | The categories of recipients, including recipients in third countries |
| (e) | Transfers of personal data to a third country, and the documentation of suitable safeguards |
| (f) | Where possible, the envisaged time limits for erasure of the different categories of data |
| (g) | Where possible, a general description of the technical and organisational security measures |

### Inputs

The generator reads a compliance repo (see [compliance-repo.md](../../spec/compliance-repo.md)) and two values given by its caller:

- `generated_at`: an RFC 3339 date-time, **given by the caller**. The generator never reads the clock.
- the compliance repo: `compliance.yaml`, `processings/*/processing.yaml`, `services/<repo>/manifest.yaml` and `sync.yaml`, `declared/<tool>/manifest.yaml`.

#### Scope of a processing

For a processing `P`, each entry of `P.services` resolves to a manifest: `repo: <r>` to `services/<r>/manifest.yaml`, `tool: <t>` to the `path` of the `declared_tools` entry `<t>` in `compliance.yaml`. The entry's **scope** is the set of `personal_data` ids of that manifest named by its `data` filter, or all of them when `data` is absent. Below, "in scope" always means "of an entry of `P.services`, within its scope":

- a `personal_data` item is in scope when its id is in the entry's scope;
- a `storage` item is in scope when its `data` list shares at least one id with the entry's scope;
- a `processors` or `outbound_flows` item (the recipients) is in scope, when the entry has `recipients`, if and only if its id is in that list (`recipients: []` means none). Without `recipients`, it is in scope when its `data` list shares at least one id with the entry's scope; a processor with no `data` is in scope.

Nothing else of the manifests is read: `automated_decisions` and `ai_features` are not part of Art. 30. `service.controller` is not read either.

The generation **fails** (the generator reports it and writes nothing) when a `repo` of `P.services` has no `services/<r>/manifest.yaml` or no `sync.yaml`, when a `tool` is not in `compliance.yaml`, or when its manifest file is missing. A register that silently omits a service would look complete.

### Mapping

Every row of a section is built as `(item, value, provenance)`. In the table, "m." is the manifest of an entry of `P.services`.

| Item | Rendered value | Source fields | Merge rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Controller | `<name>, <contact>` | `processing.controller.name`, `.contact` | One row. |
| (a) Joint controller | `To complete` | none in v0.1 | Always `To complete`. |
| (a) Representative | `To complete` | none in v0.1 | Always `To complete`. |
| (a) Data protection officer | `<name>, <contact>` | `processing.dpo.name`, `.contact` | One row; `To complete` when `dpo` is absent. |
| (b) Purposes | the text | `processing.purpose` | One row. The purposes of the manifests' `personal_data` items are not listed: a data set shared with another processing would bring that processing's purpose here. |
| (c) Data subjects | the text | `processing.data_subjects[]`; m. `personal_data[].subjects[]` | Union, de-duplicated, sorted. |
| (c) Personal data | `<category>: <name>, <name>` | m. `personal_data[].fields[]` (`category`, `name`) | One row per (in-scope `personal_data` item, category): the field names of that item in that category, de-duplicated, sorted. Rows with the same value and the same source are merged. Rows are sorted by value, then by source. |
| (d) Recipients | `<name> (<country>)` | m. `processors[].name`, `.country`; m. `outbound_flows[].to.name`, `.to.country` (both filtered by `services[].recipients`); `processing.recipients[].name` | Union of the three, see below. Sorted. |
| (e) Transfers | `<name> (<country>): <safeguard>` | m. `processors[].transfer_outside_eu`, `.safeguard`, `.country`; m. `outbound_flows[].to.country` | See below. Sorted. |
| (f) Erasure time limits | `<duration>[, <trigger>]` | `processing.retention`; m. `storage[].retention` | `processing.retention` wins: when present it is the only row. Otherwise one row per in-scope `storage` item. |
| (g) Security measures | the text | `processing.security_measures[].measure` | Sorted. The manifests state no measure. |

`compliance.yaml` is also read: `organisation.name` fills the preface, `repos[].name` and `declared_tools[]` resolve the entries of `P.services`.

#### Recipients (d)

1. For each in-scope processor: `<name> (<country>)`.
2. For each in-scope outbound flow: `<to.name> (<to.country>)`, with `country unknown` written `(country unknown)`. **Except** when `to.name` is equal to the `service.name` of a manifest of the compliance repo: that flow stays inside the information system and is not a recipient.
3. For each `processing.recipients[]`: its `name`, **except** when it names a recipient of 1 or 2: equal to its `<name>` or starting with `<name> (` (so `TextFlow SMS (SMS reminders)` is the same recipient as `TextFlow SMS`).
4. A value that comes from several items is one row (see Provenance).

#### Transfers (e)

A transfer exists for a recipient (same `<name>` and `<country>`) when:

- an in-scope processor has `transfer_outside_eu: true`, whatever its country; or
- an in-scope outbound flow has a `to.country` that is neither in the EU/EEA list below nor `unknown`.

The value is `<name> (<country>): <safeguard>`, where `<safeguard>` is the `safeguard` of the in-scope processor of the same name and country, else `safeguard not declared`. A flow whose `to.country` is `unknown` gives `<name> (country unknown): destination to assess`. No transfer found: the single row `None identified in the sources`.

EU/EEA country codes (EU member states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway), fixed by this version of the template; a change of the list is a change of the template version:

`AT BE BG HR CY CZ DK EE FI FR DE GR HU IE IS IT LV LI LT LU MT NL NO PL PT RO SK SI ES SE`

A country outside the list is a third country, even when an adequacy decision exists: the adequacy decision is the safeguard, written in the `safeguard` field.

#### Erasure time limits (f)

- With `processing.retention`: one row `<duration>[, <trigger>]`.
- Without: one row per in-scope `storage` item, `<storage id> (<kind>): <duration>[, <trigger>]`.
- `<duration>` is written as in the source: an ISO 8601 duration (`P3Y`), `none`, `session` or `unknown`. No conversion. `unknown` stays `unknown`: a reviewer treats it as open.
- No `processing.retention` and no in-scope `storage`: `To complete`.

### Provenance

The Source cell holds exactly one string per row:

| Form | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| `code: <repo>@<sha7>:<ref>` | a manifest item with `source.kind: code`. `<repo>` is the folder name under `services/`; `<sha7>` is the first 7 characters of `sha` in `services/<repo>/sync.yaml`; `<ref>` is `source.ref`, verbatim. |
| `spec: <url>` | an item with `source.kind: spec`. `<url>` is `source.ref`, verbatim. |
| `declared: <ref>` | a value of `processing.yaml`: `<ref>` is the `source.ref` of the value, or the file's `source.ref` when the value has none of its own (`controller`, `dpo`, `purpose`, `data_subjects`). |
| `declared: <file> (<ref>)` | a manifest item with `source.kind: declared`. `<file>` is the path of the manifest in the compliance repo (`services/<repo>/manifest.yaml` or `declared/<tool>/manifest.yaml`), `<ref>` is `source.ref`. |
| `—` | no source: the value is `To complete` or `None identified in the sources`. |

A `code` item found in a declared tool manifest (it has no sync) is written in the `declared: <file> (<ref>)` form. In a `processing.yaml`, `kind: spec` is written `spec: <url>` and `kind: code` is written `declared: <ref>` (a processing file has no sync).

When one **atomic** value (a text stated identically by several items: subjects, purposes, recipients, transfers) comes from several items, there is one row and one source: the strongest, ordered by kind (`code`, then `spec`, then `declared`), then by ascending source string. A **composite** value (personal data) is built from one item, so the same value from two items gives two rows.

### Missing values

A value no source gives is `To complete`, with the source `—`. It is never invented, never guessed from another field. A search that finds nothing is not a missing value: recipients and transfers give `None identified in the sources`, also with `—`. Together with `To complete`, it is the only value that goes with `—`.

Always `To complete` in v0.1: joint controller and representative (the processing format has no field for them). Possibly: DPO, erasure time limits, security measures, recipients in a section.

A person who does not know a required text value of `processing.yaml` yet may write `to complete` (any case) in it: `controller.name`, `controller.contact`, `dpo.name` or `dpo.contact`. The generator writes it `To complete`, and the row is an open item. The source of the row does not change.

### Open items

Gaps are expected: processings and manifests are filled while people work, before every answer is known. The register keeps each gap visible and lists them first, so that a review starts with what is missing.

An **open item** of a processing is one of these, found in its section:

| Open item | Found when |
| --- | --- |
| `<item>` | the item has no source and reads `To complete`; for (a) Controller and (a) Data protection officer, also when a name or contact is written `to complete` |
| `(c) Personal data: category unknown` | an in-scope `personal_data` field has the category `unknown` |
| `(e) Transfers: safeguard not declared for <name> (<country>)` | a transfer exists for a recipient and no in-scope processor of the same name and country declares a `safeguard` |
| `(e) Transfers: destination to assess for <name> (country unknown)` | an in-scope outbound flow has `to.country: unknown` |
| `(f) Erasure time limits: unknown` | the `processing.retention` duration is `unknown` |
| `(f) Erasure time limits: unknown for <storage id>` | without `processing.retention`, an in-scope storage has the retention duration `unknown` |

Open items come from the source fields, never from the rendered text: a purpose, a name or a safeguard whose words look like a gap ("To complete our offers", "SCC, safeguard not declared") is a value.

`<item>` is the item label of the table above, for example `(g) Security measures`. Joint controller and representative are **not** open items: they are always `To complete` in v0.1 and could never be closed, which would hide the items that can. The section says so in one sentence.

The open items of a processing are listed in the order of their rows, each once, joined by `; `; a processing with none reads `None`. The count is the number of open items of all processings.

The summary line is:

- `No processing activity yet.` when there is no processing;
- `No open item in the <K> processing activities.` (`activity` when K is 1) when the count is 0;
- otherwise `<N> open item(s) in <M> of the <K> processing activities.`, with `item` when N is 1 and `activity` when K is 1, M being the number of processings with at least one open item.

None identified in the sources (recipients, transfers) is not an open item: the search ran and found nothing.

### Determinism

The same inputs give the same bytes.

- Text encoding UTF-8, line endings `\n`, no trailing whitespace, exactly one `\n` at the end of the file.
- Processings are sorted by `id`. The 11 items are always in the order of the table above, and always present.
- Values are sorted in ascending order of their UTF-16 code units (JavaScript `a < b`), with no locale. Where a rule says "sorted", that is the order; no other order is used.
- Row labels: the item label is on the first row of the item, the Item cell is empty on the others.
- A cell is on one line: a newline in a value becomes a space, `|` becomes `\|`, the value is trimmed.
- `generated_at` is copied from the caller's input as it is, without reformatting. Nothing else depends on a date or on the clock.
- Anchors are sorted by repo name.

### Output front matter

```yaml
---
spec_version: "0.1"
generated_at: "2026-11-11T00:00:00Z"
open_items: 2
anchors:
  bistro-api: "7c1e4f9a2b6d8035e1a4c7f90b2d6e8a1c3f5b79"
  bistro-web: "9d3f6b1a4c7e0258b1d4f7a0c3e6b9d2f5a8c1e4"
---
```

- `spec_version`: the template version, `"0.1"`.
- `generated_at`: the input.
- `open_items`: the count of open items, an integer (see Open items).
- `anchors`: for each repo named by a `repo` entry of any processing, the full `sha` of its `services/<repo>/sync.yaml` at generation time. It has the same content as `repos` and `generated_at` in the `anchors.yaml` that the generator writes in the same run ([anchors rules](../../spec/compliance-repo.md#anchorsyaml)). Repos no processing refers to, and declared tools, are not in it. The shas are double-quoted strings here and in `anchors.yaml` and `sync.yaml`, so that a sha made only of digits and `e` is never read as a number; the generator writes them quoted.

### Template syntax

[`register.md.tmpl`](register.md.tmpl) uses three constructs. The generator builds a context and replaces them by hand.

| Construct | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `{{name}}` | The value `name` of the current context, as text. Looked up in the innermost block first, then outwards. A missing name is a generator error. |
| `{{#each name}}` ... `{{/each}}` | Repeats the block for each element of the list `name`, in the list's order; the element is the current context. Blocks nest. |
| a line that holds only a block tag | Removed entirely, with its newline. |

No escaping, no conditions, no other tag. The values are escaped as said in Determinism before they are put in the context. After rendering, trailing blank lines are reduced to one final `\n`.

Context:

| Name | Where | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `spec_version` | root | `"0.1"` (without the quotes) |
| `generated_at` | root | the input |
| `organisation` | root | `compliance.yaml` `organisation.name` |
| `open_items` | root | the count of open items |
| `open_summary` | root | the summary line of the open items |
| `open` | root | list of `{name, items}`, one per processing in the order of `processings`: `name` is `<name> (<id>)`, `items` the open items joined by `; ` or `None` |
| `anchors` | root | list of `{repo, sha}`, sorted by `repo` |
| `processings` | root | list of `{id, name, rows}`, sorted by `id` |
| `rows` | in a processing | the flat list of `{item, value, source}` of the 11 items in order |

Two special cases:

- With no anchor, the generator writes `anchors: {}` in place of the `anchors:` block.
- An empty `{{name}}` between two spaces leaves a single space, so a continuation row renders `| | value | source |`, not `|  | value | source |`.

### Limits of v0.1

- A shared `personal_data` id (`guest-contact` of Bistro Booking is used by two processings) brings the storages that hold it into every processing that uses it, and, without `services[].recipients`, the processors and flows that touch it. Recipients and transfers are then over-reported rather than missed; a processing file narrows them with `recipients`. Storages cannot be narrowed.
- One source per atomic value: the other items that state the same value are not shown. Enough for v0.1.
- No joint controller, representative, or "none" statement can be declared by a person.

## v0.2: the compliance dossier

The dossier a DPO reviews: the organisation's identity, the actions that close the open items, the records of processing (Art. 30(1)) and the DPO checks of each processing activity, the AI features, and what stays outside the register. Everything below is normative for a v0.2 compliance repo. Where a rule says "as in v0.1", the [v0.1 rule](#v01-the-records-of-processing) applies unchanged.

### Inputs

- `generated_at`: an RFC 3339 date-time, given by the caller, as in v0.1. The generator never reads the clock.
- `language`: `en` (default) or `fr`, given by the caller. It picks the template ([`register.en.md.tmpl`](register.en.md.tmpl) or [`register.fr.md.tmpl`](register.fr.md.tmpl)) and the fixed strings of [Translations](#translations). The output path is `documents/register.md` in both languages. The language is the generator's input if given, else `organisation.language` of `compliance.yaml` (v0.2.1), else `en`.
- The compliance repo: `compliance.yaml` (v0.2), `processings/*/processing.yaml`, `services/<repo>/manifest.yaml` and `sync.yaml`, `declared/<tool>/manifest.yaml`. Processing files and manifests may be v0.1 or v0.2: a field that a v0.1 file cannot hold is read as absent.

### Files read, and failures

- **Processings**: every `processings/<id>/processing.yaml`, sorted by `id`.
- **Referenced manifests**: the manifests of the entries of `services` of every processing, resolved as in v0.1 ([Scope of a processing](#scope-of-a-processing)). The scope rules of v0.1 apply, with one addition: a processor named by the `processor` of an in-scope outbound flow is in scope (see [Recipients](#recipients-d-v02)).
- **All manifests**: for [Outside the register](#outside-the-register), every repo of `compliance.yaml` `repos` that has a `services/<repo>/` folder, and every `declared_tools` entry.
- **Notices**: each entry of `information.notices` resolves to the `notices` item with that `id` in the manifest of its `repo` (a repo of `compliance.yaml`) or `tool` (a `declared_tools` id).
- **Not read yet**: `actions.yaml` and the `review` of a processing file (v0.2.1, reserved; see [compliance-repo.md](../../spec/compliance-repo.md)). They change nothing in the dossier.

The generation **fails** (the generator reports it and writes nothing) in the v0.1 cases, and also when: a repo with a `services/<repo>/` folder has no `manifest.yaml` or no `sync.yaml`; a declared tool's manifest file is missing; a notice reference does not resolve (unknown repo or tool, missing manifest, or no notice with that id). A dossier that silently drops a service or a notice would look complete.

### Structure

The document is the template filled with the context of [Template context](#template-context-v02). In order:

1. Front matter (see [Front matter](#front-matter-v02)).
2. Title, organisation name, `generated_at`, the preface and the disclaimer: fixed text of the template.
3. `## Organisation`: the identity table, four rows (see [Organisation](#organisation)).
4. `## Actions`: the summary sentence, then one row per action (see [Actions](#actions)).
5. One section per processing, sorted by `id`, titled `## <name> (<id>)` (both values verbatim), with two tables: `### Records of processing, GDPR Art. 30(1)` (the 11 items, see [Records of processing](#records-of-processing-v02)) and `### DPO checks` (the 6 checks, see [DPO checks](#dpo-checks)).
6. `## AI features`: the summary sentence, then one row per AI feature (see [AI features](#ai-features)).
7. `## Outside the register`: the summary sentence, then one row per service or component not controlled by the organisation (see [Outside the register](#outside-the-register)).
8. `## Not covered`: a fixed closing paragraph.

There is no Open items section in v0.2: open items are counted and grouped by the action that closes them.

### Values, placeholders and `to complete`

- Values written by people or found in code are never translated: names, purposes, references, free texts, and the values of the format's enums (`contract`, `yes`, `to-assess`, `limited`, `user-of-the-app`...). Dates are as in the source; countries are the ISO code of the source.
- **Placeholders** are fixed strings: `To complete` (no source gives the value), `None identified in the sources` (a search ran and found nothing), and `Not expected for this legal basis` (see [DPO checks](#dpo-checks)). In the three-column tables (Organisation, records of processing, DPO checks), a Value cell that is exactly a placeholder has the source `—`, and only such a cell has it. In the AI features and Outside the register tables, the Source cell is always the source of the row's item, whatever placeholders its other cells hold.
- **`to complete`**: a person who does not know a text value yet may write `to complete` (any case, surrounding spaces ignored) in these fields: `organisation.controller.*`, `organisation.dpo.name` and `.contact`, `organisation.representative.name` and `.contact`, `organisation.privacy_contact`; in a processing file `controller.name` and `.contact`, `dpo.name` and `.contact`, `joint_controllers[].name`, `.contact` and `.arrangement`, every text of `legal_basis_detail`, `dpia.ref`. The generator writes `To complete` in place of that value, inside the rendered value; the source of the row does not change, and the row is an open item of the item's kind (see [Open items](#open-items-v02)).
- Fixed words inside a value (`Not designated`, `DPA`, `Required`...) are translated; see [Translations](#translations).
- Value patterns use `: `, `, `, `; ` and parentheses as written below, in both languages. French typography (a space before `:` and `;`) applies only to the fixed sentences of the French template and to the French labels.

### Provenance (v0.2)

The forms of v0.1 ([Provenance](#provenance)), plus one:

| Form | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| `declared: compliance.yaml` | a value of `compliance.yaml`: the organisation's controller, DPO, representative and privacy contact, and the values a processing inherits from them. |

A notice is a manifest item: its source is written like any other (`code: <repo>@<sha7>:<ref>`, `spec: <url>`, `declared: <file> (<ref>)`). A value of a processing file without a `source` of its own takes the file's `source.ref`: `declared: <ref>`.

Atomic values (one row, one source, the strongest, as in v0.1): data subjects, recipients, transfers, processor contracts, notices. Composite values: personal data, as in v0.1.

### Organisation

Four rows, always, in this order. Source: `declared: compliance.yaml`, or `—` with a placeholder.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Controller | `organisation.controller`: `<legal_name>, <address>, <registration>, <contact>`, `address` and `registration` left out with their `, ` when absent. Absent: `To complete`. |
| Data protection officer | `organisation.dpo`: `designated: true`: `<name>, <contact>`; `designated: false`: `Not designated: <reason>`; `designated: unknown`, or no `dpo`: `To complete`. |
| Representative (Art. 27) | `organisation.representative`: `required: true`: `<name>, <contact>`; `required: false`: `Not required`, followed by `: <reason>` when `reason` is present; `required: unknown`, or no `representative`: `To complete`. |
| Privacy contact | `organisation.privacy_contact`. Absent: `To complete`. |

Only the representative can be an open item here (see [Open items](#open-items-v02)). The controller and the DPO are counted in each processing that uses them, since a processing may state its own; the privacy contact is shown, never counted.

### Records of processing (v0.2)

The 11 items of v0.1 ([Mapping](#mapping)), in the same order, with the same labels, built as in v0.1 except:

| Item | v0.2 rule |
| --- | --- |
| (a) Controller | `processing.controller` present: `<name>, <contact>`, source `declared: <processing source.ref>`. Absent: the Organisation Controller value, source `declared: compliance.yaml`. Neither: `To complete`. |
| (a) Joint controller | `joint_controllers` absent: `To complete`. `[]`: `None`, source `declared: <processing source.ref>`. Otherwise one row per entry, `<name>, <contact> (arrangement: <arrangement>)`, source `declared: <processing source.ref>`, sorted by value. |
| (a) Representative | The Organisation Representative value and source. Never an open item here. |
| (a) Data protection officer | `processing.dpo` present: `<name>, <contact>`, source `declared: <processing source.ref>`. Absent: the Organisation Data protection officer value and source (including `Not designated: <reason>`, and `To complete`). |
| (c) Personal data | As in v0.1. The category `connection` is written like any other. |
| (d) Recipients | See [Recipients](#recipients-d-v02). |
| (e) Transfers | See [Transfers](#transfers-e-v02). |

(b) Purposes, (c) Data subjects, (f) Erasure time limits and (g) Security measures are built as in v0.1.

#### Recipients (d) v0.2

A recipient is a processor, or a flow that is not a processor. Each in-scope item gives at most one row:

1. An in-scope processor: `<name> (<country>): DPA <dpa>`, with the processor's source.
2. An in-scope outbound flow **linked** to a processor gives the row of rule 1 for that processor, with the processor's source. A flow is linked to processor `X` of the same manifest when its `processor` is `X`'s id, or, when it has no `processor`, when its `to.name` and `to.country` are equal to `X`'s `name` and `country` (the v0.1 matching, kept for v0.1 manifests and unlinked flows).
3. Any other in-scope outbound flow: `<to.name> (<to.country>)`, or `<to.name> (country unknown)`, with the flow's source. **Except** when `to.name` is equal to the `service.name` of a manifest of the compliance repo, as in v0.1.
4. Each `processing.recipients[]`: its `name`, with its source, **except** when it names a recipient of rules 1 to 3: equal to that recipient's name (processor `name` or flow `to.name`), or starting with that name followed by ` (`.
5. Equal values are one row (atomic), sorted. No row: `None identified in the sources`.

A processor linked from an in-scope flow is in scope for (d), (e) and the processor contracts, even when the entry's `recipients` does not name it.

#### Transfers (e) v0.2

Each in-scope item gives at most one row:

1. An in-scope processor with `transfer_outside_eu: true`: `<name> (<country>): <safeguard>`, where `<safeguard>` is its `safeguard`, else `safeguard not declared`. Source: the processor's.
2. An in-scope flow linked to processor `X` (as in [Recipients](#recipients-d-v02)), when `X.transfer_outside_eu` is `true` or `X.country` is not in the EU/EEA list of v0.1: `<X.name> (<X.country>): <safeguard>`, where `<safeguard>` is the flow's `safeguard`, else `X.safeguard`, else `safeguard not declared`. Source: the flow's when its own `safeguard` is used, else `X`'s.
3. An in-scope flow not linked to a processor, whose `to.country` is `unknown`: `<to.name> (country unknown): <safeguard>` when the flow has a `safeguard`, else `<to.name> (country unknown): destination to assess`. Source: the flow's.
4. An in-scope flow not linked to a processor, whose `to.country` is not in the EU/EEA list: `<to.name> (<to.country>): <safeguard>`, the flow's `safeguard`, else `safeguard not declared`. Source: the flow's.
5. Equal values are one row (atomic), sorted. No row: `None identified in the sources`.

`safeguard not declared` therefore appears only when neither the flow nor its linked processor has a safeguard.

### DPO checks

Six checks, always, in this order, one or more rows each; the Check cell is filled on the first row of a check and empty on the others. A source `declared: <P>` below is `declared: <processing source.ref>`.

| Check | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| Legal basis | `<legal_basis>`, `declared: <P>`. |
| Legal basis detail | According to `legal_basis`: `legitimate-interests`: four rows `Interest: <interest>`, `Necessity: <necessity>`, `Impact: <impact>`, `Safeguards: <safeguards>` from `legal_basis_detail.balancing_test`; `consent`: two rows `Collection: <collection>`, `Withdrawal: <withdrawal>` from `legal_basis_detail.consent`; `contract`, `legal-obligation`, `public-task`, `vital-interests`: one row `Reference: <reference>` from `legal_basis_detail.reference`. Source `declared: <P>`. The expected part absent (whatever else `legal_basis_detail` holds): one row `To complete`. `not-applicable`: one row `Not expected for this legal basis`. |
| DPIA | `dpia` absent: `To complete`. Otherwise one row, `declared: <P>`: `Required: <required>`, then `; Criteria: <criteria>` when `criteria` is present (the criteria sorted, joined by `, `; `None` when `[]`), then `; Reference: <ref>` when `ref` is present, or `; Reference: To complete` when `required` is `yes` and `ref` is absent. |
| Information of persons | `information` absent: `To complete`. Otherwise one row per notice of `information.notices`: `<kind>: <ref> (<service>)`, where `<service>` is the repo name or the tool id, with the notice's source; equal values are one row, sorted. Then, when `text` is present, one row `<text>`, `declared: <P>`. |

### AI features

One row per item of `ai_features` of every **referenced** manifest (a manifest of an entry of `services` of at least one processing), each manifest once. Rows are sorted by Service, then by feature `id`. Source: the feature's.

| Column | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Service | The repo name, or the declared tool id. |
| Feature | `<id>: <description>`. |
| Role | `role`; absent: `To complete`. |
| Risk | `risk.level`, followed by `: <reason>` when `risk.reason` is present; `risk` absent: `To complete`. |
| Obligations | The listed obligations and the expected obligations that are not listed, sorted by `id`, joined by `; `. A listed one: `<id>: <status>`, followed by ` (<ref>)` when `ref` is present. An expected one that is not listed: `<id>: To complete`. |

The **expected** obligations of a feature are those of the table in [manifest.md](../../spec/manifest.md#ai_features): `art4-ai-literacy` always; `art50-interaction` when `interacts_with_persons` is `true`; `art50-synthetic-content` when `generates_content` is `true`; `art9-risk-management` to `art15-accuracy-robustness` when `risk.level` is `high`; `art26-deployer-duties` and `art27-fria` when `risk.level` is `high` and `role` is `deployer`. `art50-deepfake` and `art50-emotion-biometric` are never expected: they are listed by a person when they apply. An obligation listed with `not-applicable` is listed.

The summary sentence counts the rows (see [Translations](#translations)).

### Outside the register

What the organisation does not control, with why. Read from **all** manifests (see [Files read](#files-read-and-failures)). For each manifest, in this order:

1. When `service.controller` is not `organisation`: one row for the service. Its personal data are the `personal_data` items whose controller is `service.controller`: items without `component`, and items of a component whose `controller` is absent or equal to `service.controller`.
2. For each component whose `controller` is present, not `organisation`, and different from `service.controller`: one row for the component. Its personal data are the `personal_data` items of that component.

| Column | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Service | Service row: the repo name or the tool id. Component row: `<repo or tool>/<component id>`. |
| Controller | The controller of the row: `service.controller` or the component's `controller`. |
| Rationale | `service.controller_rationale`; absent: `To complete`. |
| Privacy by design | `service.privacy_by_design`, sorted, joined by `; `; absent: `None identified in the sources`. |
| Personal data | One `<id> (<categories>)` per personal data item of the row, the distinct categories of its fields sorted and joined by `, `; the items sorted by id, joined by `; `. None: `None identified in the sources`. |
| Source | `service.source`, written as any manifest item's source. |

Rows are sorted by Service. The rationale is per service: a service with several rows has one rationale, and one open item when it is missing.

### Open items (v0.2)

An open item is detected from the **source fields**, never from the rendered text: a purpose, a name or a safeguard whose words look like a gap ("To complete our offers", "SCC, safeguard not declared") is a value. Each open item has a **place** (`Organisation`, a processing id, `AI features`, `Outside the register`), a **kind** (its action id) and a **subject**. Open items with the same place, kind and subject are one open item: an item is counted once per place it appears.

| Kind (action id) | Place | Subject | Detected from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `organisation-identity` | processing | the controller | (a) Controller is `To complete` (no `processing.controller` and no `organisation.controller`), or a value of the controller it uses is written `to complete` |
| `organisation-dpo` | processing | the DPO | (a) Data protection officer is `To complete` (no `processing.dpo`, and `organisation.dpo` absent or `designated: unknown`), or a value of the DPO it uses is written `to complete` |
| `organisation-representative` | Organisation | the representative | `organisation.representative` absent or `required: unknown`, or with `required: true`, a `name` or `contact` written `to complete` |
| `joint-controllers` | processing | the joint controllers | `joint_controllers` absent (a v0.1 processing has none), or a value of an entry written `to complete` |
| `personal-data` | processing | `To complete`, or `unknown` | (c) Personal data is `To complete` (no in-scope `personal_data`); or an in-scope field has the category `unknown` (one open item per processing, whatever the number of fields) |
| `transfer-safeguards` | processing | the recipient `<name> (<country>)` | a transfer row whose safeguard is `safeguard not declared`, or `destination to assess` |
| `processor-contracts` | processing | the processor `<name> (<country>)` | an in-scope processor with `dpa: no` or `dpa: unknown` |
| `retention` | processing | the processing, or a storage id | as in v0.1: (f) is `To complete`; `processing.retention.duration` is `unknown`; or, without `processing.retention`, an in-scope storage has the duration `unknown` (one per storage) |
| `security-measures` | processing | the processing | (g) is `To complete` |
| `legal-basis-detail` | processing | the processing | the detail expected for `legal_basis` is absent, or one of its texts is written `to complete` |
| `dpia` | processing | the processing | `dpia` absent; `required: to-assess`; or `required: yes` with `ref` absent or written `to complete` |
| `information` | processing | the processing, or a notice `<service>/<id>` | `information` absent; or a notice it names has `kind: none` (one per notice) |
| `sensitive-data` | processing | the processing | `sensitive_data` absent or `to-assess` |
| `ai-classification` | AI features | `<service>/<feature id>` and `role` or `risk` | `role` absent or `to-assess`; `risk` absent, `level: to-assess` or `level: prohibited` (a prohibited practice is reviewed first) |
| `ai-obligations` | AI features | `<service>/<feature id>` and the obligation id | an obligation with `status: open`; an expected obligation that is not listed |
| `controller-rationale` | Outside the register | the service | a service with at least one row in Outside the register and no `controller_rationale` |

Not open items: a placeholder `None identified in the sources` (the search ran); the Organisation Controller, Data protection officer and Privacy contact rows (counted in the processings that use them, or never); (a) Representative in a processing (counted once, in Organisation); `dpa: yes` without `dpa_ref`; `dpia.required: no`.

`open_items` in the front matter is the number of open items.

### Determinism (v0.2)

The rules of v0.1 ([Determinism](#determinism)) apply: UTF-8, `\n`, no trailing whitespace, one final `\n`; values sorted by UTF-16 code units (JavaScript `a < b`), with no locale, in both languages; a cell on one line (newline to space, `|` to `\|`, trimmed); `generated_at` copied as given; nothing depends on the clock. And:

- Processings are sorted by `id`; the 11 items and the 6 checks are always present, in their order.
- Every list is sorted as its rule says; "sorted" without more is ascending by value. Sorting is done on the final string, after `To complete` is put in place and before escaping.
- Actions are sorted by role order, count (descending), id; AI features by service then id; Outside the register rows by Service.
- The same inputs and the same `language` give the same bytes. The two languages give documents with the same lines, except the lines of fixed text and the cells that hold fixed strings; the Source cells are identical.

### Front matter (v0.2)

```yaml
---
spec_version: "0.2"
language: en
generated_at: "2026-11-11T00:00:00Z"
open_items: 15
actions: 14
anchors:
  bistro-api: "7c1e4f9a2b6d8035e1a4c7f90b2d6e8a1c3f5b79"
  bistro-web: "9d3f6b1a4c7e0258b1d4f7a0c3e6b9d2f5a8c1e4"
---
```

- `spec_version`: `"0.2"`. `language`: `en` or `fr`. `generated_at`: the input.
- `open_items`: the number of open items. `actions`: the number of actions (rows of the Actions table). Both integers; `open_items` is the sum of the action counts.
- `anchors`: for every repo of `compliance.yaml` that has a `services/<repo>/` folder (every repo whose manifest the dossier reads), the full `sha` of its `sync.yaml`, double-quoted, sorted by repo name. With none, `anchors: {}` in place of the block. The generator writes `anchors.yaml` in the same run with the same content and `spec_version: "0.2"` ([anchors rules](../../spec/compliance-repo.md#anchorsyaml)).

### Golden files

`examples/bistro-booking/documents/register.md` (`language: en`) and `examples/bistro-booking/documents/register.fr.md` (`language: fr`) are generated by compliance-action from `examples/bistro-booking/` with `generated_at: "2026-11-11T00:00:00Z"`, and copied here byte for byte. The tests of this repo check their structure against these rules when they are present.

The Bistro Booking example is built so that most kinds of open item show: the representative is `unknown`; `table-bookings` fills every DPO check and has no open item; `marketing-emails` leaves the joint controllers, the legal basis detail, the DPIA, the information, the sensitive data, the security measures and a retention open, uses a processor with `dpa: "no"` and a transfer without safeguard, and holds a field of category `unknown`; the no-show score is `limited` with one open obligation, the booking assistant has its role to assess and misses `art50-synthetic-content`; `bistro-web` has an offline app controlled by its user, with a rationale, and the CRM (a v0.1 manifest, `shared`) has none. The controller and the DPO are complete, so `organisation-identity` and `organisation-dpo` do not show.

### Limits of v0.2

- Inheritance covers the controller and the DPO only. A processing cannot state its own representative.
- Component controllers are shown in Outside the register; they do not change the Art. 30 table, which follows the processing's scope.
- The expected AI Act obligations are computed from the flags, the level and the role only; `art50-deepfake` and `art50-emotion-biometric` rely on a person.
- Enum values are written as in the source, in both languages.
